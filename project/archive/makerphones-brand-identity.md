# MAKER PHONES - Brand Identity Document

## Brand Overview

**Brand Name:** Maker Phones  
**Tagline:** Build Your Own Sound  
**Mission:** Make DIY headphone design accessible to anyone with curiosity and a 3D printer by providing comprehensive education and resources that don't currently exist.

**Brand Positioning Statement:**  
"Professional audio knowledge made accessible to makers - backed by 25+ years of industry experience, designed for curious builders."

### Logo

- Primary: "Maker Phones" in brand font

### Typography

#### Headings
**Font Family:** System font stack for performance
- Primary: `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "Segoe UI", Roboto, "Helvetica Neue", Arial, sans-serif`
- Weight: 700 (Bold) for H1-H2, 600 (Semi-bold) for H3-H6
- Style: Clean, modern, highly readable

**Hierarchy:**
- H1: 2.5rem (40px) - Page titles
- H2: 2rem (32px) - Section headers
- H3: 1.5rem (24px) - Subsections
- H4: 1.25rem (20px) - Minor headings
- H5-H6: 1rem (16px) - Small headings

#### Body Text
**Font Family:** Same system stack for consistency
- Weight: 400 (Regular) for body, 500 (Medium) for emphasis
- Size: 1rem (16px) base
- Line height: 1.6 (for readability)
- Paragraph spacing: 1em between paragraphs

#### Code/Technical
**Font Family:** `"SF Mono", "Monaco", "Inconsolata", "Roboto Mono", monospace`
- Usage: Code snippets, technical specifications, measurements
- Background: Light gray (#f3f4f6) for inline code
- Padding: Small padding for readability

### Design Principles

#### Layout
**Clean/Minimal with Purpose**
- Generous whitespace (breathing room for content)
- Clear visual hierarchy
- Occasional rich visual content (diagrams, photos, videos)
- Grid-based layout for consistency
- Mobile-first responsive design

#### Content Structure
- Short paragraphs (3-5 sentences max)
- Subheadings every 2-3 paragraphs
- Bulleted lists only when necessary (avoid over-formatting)
- Images/diagrams to support learning
- Video embeds for complex processes

#### Imagery Style
**Photography:**
- Natural workshop lighting
- Authentic, not staged
- Show process and tools
- Hands working = personality
- Close-ups of components for detail
- Wide shots for context

**Diagrams/Illustrations:**
- Clean, minimal line art
- Annotated with clear labels
- Use brand colors (charcoal + orange highlights)
- White or off-white backgrounds
- Consistent style across all diagrams

**Stock Photo Guidelines (Placeholders):**
- Prefer authentic over glossy
- Workbench/maker aesthetic over corporate
- Natural colors, avoid oversaturation
- Technology/audio themes
- Credit when required

## Brand Voice & Tone

### Voice Characteristics

**Authentic**
- Real experience, real failures, real learning
- "Here's what I learned" not "You must do this"
- Honest about limitations and trade-offs
- Share both successes and struggles

**Knowledgeable but Approachable**
- Professional expertise without jargon overload
- Explain technical terms clearly
- Assume curiosity, not existing knowledge
- "Let me break this down" energy

**Enthusiastic**
- Genuine excitement about DIY audio
- "This is cool and you can do it too"
- Encouraging, never condescending
- Celebrate community wins

**Conversational**
- Write like you're talking to a friend in the workshop
- Contractions are fine (we're, you'll, that's)
- Direct address ("you" and "we")
- Questions to engage reader

### Tone Spectrum

**Website Content:** Casual-Professional (7/10 casual)
- Clean language, conversational structure
- Can say "badass" or "damn good"
- Avoid excessive profanity
- Friendly but informative

**Videos (YouTube):** Very Casual (9/10 casual)
- Be yourself, natural speech
- If a "shit" or "damn" slips out while troubleshooting, that's authentic
- Don't script curse words, but don't censor natural reactions
- Keep it workshop-appropriate (not aggressive or excessive)

**Community Guidelines:** Friendly but Respectful
- "Keep it friendly, occasional colorful language is fine"
- "Don't be a jerk" principle
- Focus on helpfulness over politeness theater

**Email/Newsletter:** Conversational-Friendly (8/10 casual)
- Like updates from a friend
- Personal, direct
- Occasional humor
- Always valuable, never spammy

### Writing Style Guidelines

**Do:**
- ✅ Use contractions (we're, you'll, it's)
- ✅ Ask questions to engage
- ✅ Share personal anecdotes
- ✅ Admit when you don't know something
- ✅ Use analogies from everyday life
- ✅ Break complex topics into digestible pieces
- ✅ Encourage experimentation

**Don't:**
- ❌ Use marketing hype or exaggeration
- ❌ Pretend to know everything
- ❌ Talk down to beginners
- ❌ Overcomplicate simple concepts
- ❌ Use unexplained jargon
- ❌ Be preachy or dogmatic
- ❌ Copy other people's content

### Example Phrases (On Brand)

**Good:**
- "Let's dive into how this actually works"
- "Here's what I learned the hard way so you don't have to"
- "This is where it gets interesting"
- "Your first build won't be perfect—and that's totally fine"
- "I've spent 25+ years learning this stuff, so you don't need to"
- "Here's the thing nobody tells you about driver selection..."

**Avoid:**
- "Leverage synergistic solutions" ❌
- "Industry-leading performance" ❌
- "Revolutionary breakthrough" ❌
- "You absolutely must..." ❌
- "Everyone knows that..." ❌
- "Simply just..." ❌

## Content Guidelines

### Article Structure

**Every Article Should Have:**
1. Clear title (what will you learn?)
2. Difficulty indicator (Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced)
3. Time to read estimate
4. "Why This Matters" opening
5. Core content with subheadings
6. Visual aids (diagrams, photos, videos)
7. "What This Means for Your Build" practical section
8. Related articles/next steps

**Article Titles:**
- Clear and descriptive
- Action-oriented when possible
- No clickbait
- SEO-friendly but natural

Examples:
- ✅ "How Headphones Actually Work"
- ✅ "Your First DIY Headphone Build"
- ✅ "Understanding Frequency Response Graphs"
- ❌ "You Won't Believe What Happens When..."
- ❌ "The Secret Headphone Manufacturers Don't Want You to Know"

### Video Content

**Format:**
- Casual workshop style
- Natural, unscripted (outline, not script)
- Show process, including mistakes
- 5-15 minutes optimal
- Clear audio (you're an audio professional!)

**Structure:**
1. Quick intro (what we're doing today)
2. Show the work (document the process)
3. Explain the why (educational value)
4. Show results (honest assessment)
5. What's next (keep them engaged)

**YouTube Titles:**
- Descriptive but engaging
- Include key terms (DIY, headphones, build, etc.)
- Front-load important info
- Keep under 60 characters when possible

Examples:
- ✅ "Building My First 3D Printed Headphones - Full Process"
- ✅ "How to Measure Headphones on a Budget (Under $200)"
- ✅ "This Driver Choice Was a Mistake - Here's What I Learned"

## Usage Guidelines

### For Jamey/Content Creators

**When Writing:**
1. Read your draft out loud—does it sound like you talking?
2. Could a beginner understand this?
3. Did you explain the "why" not just the "how"?
4. Is this genuinely helpful or just filler?
5. Would past-you have found this valuable?

**When Creating Videos:**
1. Are you being your authentic self?
2. Is the audio quality good? (You're an audio pro!)
3. Does this show real work, not just talking?
4. Did you explain what you're doing and why?
5. Is this worth someone's 10 minutes?

**When Engaging with Community:**
1. Are you being helpful, not defensive?
2. Did you give credit where due?
3. Are you listening to feedback?
4. Did you treat them with respect?
5. Are you building trust?

- Is this authentic to who you are?
- Would this help someone learn or build?
- Does this align with "Build Your Own Sound"?
